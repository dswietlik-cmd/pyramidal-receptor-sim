# Comparison of two matched pathological mechanisms

Two moderate pathological phenotypes were compared using the same n=20 paired stochastic
input seeds and the same drug concentration grids.

1. **Input-driven hyperexcitability**: 1.65× excitatory presynaptic drive, NMDA pathology multiplier 1.0.
2. **NMDA-driven excitotoxicity-like state**: normal presynaptic frequencies, NMDA pathology multiplier 4.10.

Their mean drug-free firing rates are intentionally similar (~24 Hz), allowing a mechanism-level
comparison rather than a comparison confounded by different baseline output activity.

## Main observation

Neither mechanism produced a broad therapeutic window in which pathological firing was
strongly suppressed while ≥80% of healthy firing was preserved. However, the NMDA-driven
state was modestly more suppressible at concentrations that preserved healthy firing.

At the ≥80% healthy-firing preservation criterion, the best observed pathological suppression
within the tested grids was:

| Pathology | Drug | Concentration | Pathological suppression | Healthy firing preserved |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Input-driven | Perampanel | 8 nM | 7.6% | 82.5% |
| Input-driven | Memantine | 50 nM | 2.5% | 93.0% |
| Input-driven | Diazepam | 5 nM | 1.4% | 92.3% |
| NMDA-driven | Perampanel | 8 nM | 11.3% | 82.5% |
| NMDA-driven | Memantine | 50 nM | 6.9% | 93.0% |
| NMDA-driven | Diazepam | 5 nM | 0.6% | 92.3% |

These results are computational and should not be interpreted as clinical comparative efficacy.
They indicate that disease mechanism strongly affects the functional concentration-response
relationship in this model.
